## Photo observations

The photograph is a low, street-level oblique exterior view across a road. It compresses depth: a long receding wall connects a prominent curved bastion at the right to a taller, layered palace block behind the left side. These are image relationships, not a measured plan or proof of camera bearing.

1. **Exterior opening:** a dark pointed arch is located right of center in a lighter rectangular masonry surround; a smaller vertical element stands to its left. Depth, exact gate name, door leaves, hinges and the path beyond are not visible. Do not equate this opening with Suraj Pol until the sequence and location are independently checked.
2. **Bastions:** a large rounded red-toned bastion anchors the right edge, with curved wall surfaces and a parapet. There is another rounded or curved defensive section to the left of the entrance. Exact radius, count and full rear profile cannot be measured here.
3. **Ramparts:** the long wall has a stepped or angled upper boundary, repeated rounded merlons and regularly spaced small dark openings. Joint layout and whether all openings are original cannot be resolved at this image size.
4. **Palace above:** layered multi-storey masses rise behind the left rampart. Recessed openings, projecting balconies, parapet and small roof pavilions articulate the skyline; fine jharokha and chhatri geometry is too small for precise copying. Apparent heights are perspective-dependent.
5. **Foreground:** a separate low red boundary wall with pale inset outlines runs beside the street and interrupts the clear approach. It must not be mistaken for the fort rampart. Road, curb, vegetation, lighting poles, signs, people or vehicles are contextual elements, not fort architecture.
6. **Materials:** sunlit red/terracotta walls contrast with a pale, mottled gate frame and paler accents on the palace. Shadows and compression affect color; the photo does not establish exact stone types or a color-calibrated palette. Study stone joint, roughness and weathering from closer references.

Treat OSM coordinates and image camera alignment as unverified until independent survey and view matching.
- Reference **01** is a close oblique view labelled Suraj Pol, showing pale nested arches; **06–07** show elephant guards near that gate; **08** shows a studded timber door labelled Suraj Parole. These supplement gateway detail but do not prove that the visible exterior arch in the primary image is the same gate or establish hinge operation.
- References **02–05** show internal facades, balconies and courtyards. They inform the design vocabulary and depth of ornament, not the precise placement of the exterior palace block. Review each image's attribution and license in the inventory.
- The primary photo supports relative silhouette, visible wall/foreground separation, general parapet rhythm, arch outline and red/pale contrast. Its 800 × 500 resolution does not support measured dimensions, exact stone courses, door panels, hidden elevations, authentic ornament profiles or a complete 3D fort.
