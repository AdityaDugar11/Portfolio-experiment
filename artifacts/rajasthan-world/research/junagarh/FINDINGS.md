# Junagarh Fort — real-world asset investigation

Research date: 26 November 2026. Scope: ₹0, no new reconstruction. Existing Blender studies and 72 cached Copernicus GLO-90 tiles remain untouched. Current `.blend` studies are exploratory and were not accepted as likeness benchmarks.

## Main finding

OSM contains a useful outer fort polygon and mapped building footprints, but a downloadable, accurately modeled and reuse-cleared Junagarh 3D scan has not been verified. Eight separately credited Commons photographs provide gateway and courtyard references; they do not form a controlled overlapping capture set. A credible reconstruction requires more licensed multi-angle photography and dimensional cross-checks, ideally a permission-cleared original capture. OSM is a spatial guide, not an architectural survey.

## What the OSM extract actually shows

Way `126998139` is tagged `historic=castle`, `barrier=city_wall`, `name=Junagarh Fort`; its closed outline contains 117 coordinate entries. The selected bbox extract contains 45 `building` ways and 139 `highway` ways in total, including ways extending beyond the bbox. A simple centroid-in-polygon check placed 26 closed building footprints inside the mapped fort outline. All 26 have `building=yes` and no name. This count is not a survey: centroid filtering does not test complete polygon overlap, and mapped geometries may be approximate, duplicated, absent, or outdated. The query did not produce explicitly tagged courtyard geometry or waterway ways in the selected extract. Spaces between buildings are not proof of courtyards, and no interior room layout, height, door location or facade geometry can be inferred safely from these ways.

## Reference coverage and photogrammetry

Contact-sheet entries 01 and 06–08 show Suraj Pol, including nearby elephant-guard views and a detailed door. Entries 02–05 show fort interiors/courtyards and facade details; their exact internal positions require confirmation. None is a verified aerial survey or a measured straight-on elevation, and some photographs show different parts of the complex. External wall/bastion, aerial footprint, roofline, rear passage and consistent door-side coverage remain insufficient. The [Survey of India Bikaner map](https://surveyofindia.gov.in/files/bikaner.pdf) and [Incredible India Junagarh description](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/rajasthan/bikaner/junagarh-fort) are reference-only links; their images were not downloaded or relicensed.

[AliceVision/Meshroom](https://alicevision.org/) is open source and [COLMAP](https://colmap.github.io/) is a free reconstruction option. Neither was found on the local PATH during the prior check. Separate tourist photographs with different years, lenses, occlusions and unknown overlap cannot be assumed suitable for a reliable multi-view solve. The eight collected images are useful modeling references, not a verified photogrammetry capture. A field survey needs site permission and a consistent, high-overlap sequence around each permitted subject, scale markers/measurements, coverage of recesses and roofs where permitted, and clear rights from every photographer. The right to use an image must be established independently of the software license.

## Recommended next step for owner approval

First obtain an authorized multi-angle capture or sufficiently documented open reference collection of Suraj Pol and its immediate passage; secure a few ground-control dimensions and clarify photography and site permissions. Use the OSM outline as a provisional spatial scaffold, check it against a permitted survey or measured references, and model the gate with carefully matched profiles and depth. Use original materials or the CC0 sets only after material fit checks; acquire source imagery at adequate resolution under its terms. Test a small photogrammetry solve only when a coherent image set exists. Keep the rest of the fort represented by verified low-detail geometry until separate reference and approval. No new fort reconstruction was started during this investigation.
