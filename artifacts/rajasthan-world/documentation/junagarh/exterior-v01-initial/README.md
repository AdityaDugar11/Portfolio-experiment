# Junagarh exterior v01 — incomplete geometry study

This is an editable exploratory scene, **not a completed or historically verified reconstruction**. The primary photograph is loaded as a hidden local image empty and not packed or used as a texture. It is restricted, stored separately and ignored in Git. The `OSM_REFERENCE` collection contains the OSM `way/126998139` boundary as a hidden render guide (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL); its metric coordinates are centered and are not aligned to the modeled facade. The 26 building footprints were reviewed in the existing extract but are **not yet incorporated or matched to the architecture**. The exterior geometry is an estimated front-facing arrangement; its relationship to the actual footprint, gate identity, camera bearing and measurements remain unverified.

The scene contains a long rampart, two bastion masses, a pointed-arch opening with intrados geometry, independent door hinges/leaves, generic palace masses and foreground wall. The facade **does not yet resemble the photograph sufficiently**: the palace openings and balconies are generic, jharokhas and chhatris are missing, the reference's curved wall joins, correct battlement profile, gate recess and stone details need careful re-modeling, and the street-level composition and proportions are not matched. The supposed passage is only a floor with no modeled interior. Door hinge movement is an interpretation; collision, camera clearance and historical construction are unverified. There are no complete interior or model of the rest of the fort.

Materials are references to existing 1k Poly Haven CC0 assets `red_sandstone_wall`, `old_sandstone_02`, `fine_grained_wood`, and `dry_ground_01`; see `research/junagarh/materials/inventory.json` for authors, links and maps. Iron is an original flat material. Their scale and visual fit are unvalidated. The runtime logs OpenColorIO 2.5 versus 2.4.2 incompatibility; color management is disabled and image textures cannot reliably be converted from sRGB or Non-Color. Images are not color-accurate and should not be used to approve materials. No system library was changed.
