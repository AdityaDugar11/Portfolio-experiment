# Phase 03.3 environment pipeline and Junagarh study

## What broke in Phase 03.2.1

The original high and low stills and both ten-second Gemini clips show connected dune crests, jagged exposed strata, abrupt rock-to-scrub transitions, nested architecture and a coherent cutaway silhouette. The later clip opens with the state outline before revealing the terrain. At equivalent overview scale, Phase 03.2.1 reads as alternating stamped dunes, smoothed ridges and foliage patches. Its approach render shows the loss of scale cues most clearly. The broad ground surface, repeated settlement modules and near-uniform red wall understate masonry depth and surface variation.

The source explains the appearance. Phase 03.2 stamps and blends 69 variants of one dune profile onto a kilometre-scale grid, with smoothing and limits on slope. Phase 03.2.1 retains those landforms, adds vegetation instances and edits node materials. Regional terrain resolution, tileable pigments and repeatable plant silhouettes leave little metre-scale detail. The Phase 03.2.1 Junagarh import uses an artwork scale of 0.44 inherited units per source metre. Its horizontal scale, terrain scale and camera distance do not establish a coherent human-scale approach. Light changes leave those underlying limits in place. [Phase 03.2](../diorama-phase-03-2/README.md) · [Phase 03.2.1](../diorama-phase-03-2-1/README.md)

The benchmark builds the main dune forms from measured relief and uses independent scanned rocks with baked albedo, roughness and normals. A small sheltered strip supports talus and scrub. The crests retain open sand, and the pavilion gives both camera views a metre-scale architectural cue. The sculptural arrangement is an illustration assembled from sources listed below.

## Junagarh reconstruction

I reopened the actual exterior and the Phase 03.2.1 import. The source contains 117 visible architectural/context meshes across its first six collections, including the street. The diorama imports 116. Reusable work includes the modeled pointed portal and archivolts, pale dressed surround, barrel-vaulted passage and masonry ribs, jharokha, chhajja, pierced screens, pavilion studies and their UVs. The two animated timber leaves and attached straps and nailheads retain independent hinge origins. Both files report closed at frame 1 and about ±86° at frame 61. Preserve that implementation as an interaction study. The final door geometry and hardware depend on measured gate evidence. The twelve CC0 maps supply generic stone, timber and ground channels. Reuse suitable materials after matching texture scale and condition to actual photographs. [Exterior study and existing clearance checks](../documentation/junagarh/exterior-v01/README.md) · [Referenced material inventory](../research/junagarh/materials/inventory.json)

The façade supports the provisional profile seen from the approved street camera. The full perimeter, hidden corners, interconnected palace roofs and actual gate sequence still require evidence. The outer OSM outline and 26 unnamed internal footprints give useful plan constraints with ODbL attribution. They provide insufficient evidence to assign palace identity, wall height or entrance alignment. The prototype copies one 2.6 m-wide chhatri from the editable source as an illustrative desert pavilion on a new stone plinth. Junagarh itself sits on the Bikaner plain. 1. Cross-check the primary exterior image, named archival views and the OSM outline. Identify the visible street entrance, locate Suraj Pol independently, and record measured positions, sightlines, heights and evidence gaps. Obtain permission-cleared plans or a multi-angle capture of the chosen approach from the site custodian where available.
2. Register two or more independent views to measured control points in a local metre coordinate system. Build verified wall, bastion, moat, entry and palace silhouettes first. Check the plan from above and the frontage from both directions. Track confidence for incomplete sections.
3. Use a high-overlap permission-cleared photo set with scale references for a small COLMAP test of masonry or carvings. Retopologize usable scans and bake detail. Continue manual reconstruction for undercuts, missing surfaces and constructed profiles. Retain the existing editable gate assembly in a parallel collection until dimensions and motion can be checked together. [COLMAP](https://colmap.github.io/)
4. Review the approach against independently held-out photos. Validate the actual threshold height, swept hinge clearances and a pedestrian camera envelope. Then develop tiled materials and far, mid and near models from the reference-checked exterior. Keep unsupported wings provisional until coverage improves.

## Scaling after approval

Keep the existing LCC state boundary, ten representative fort anchors and chapter IDs as the geographic baseline. Use GLO-90 for overview and 30 m subsets only where ridge position, drainage or terrain fit needs it. Maintain true local metres for approved approach patches. Store the LCC transform and origin with each patch and stitch its outer elevations against a lower-detail surround. Use real imagery and vectors to locate dune belts, bare rock, dry scrub, riparian ground and roads. At the atlas distance, use baked relief and land cover with the surveyed shape. Close up, use the benchmark’s metre-level geometry, shared scans or measured analogues, physically scaled PBR, and deliberate placement at shelter lines, stone feet and actual ground layers. Keep geographically incorrect analogues out of the measured map layers. Build one Thar site and one Aravalli slope test before attempting region-wide treatment.

A 2K RGBA8 map with mipmaps costs about 21.3 MiB uncompressed. Three 2K maps at 8 bits per pixel of GPU block compression cost about 16 MiB with mips. Share trim sheets and use 512–1K foliage/talus maps. Check the actual transcoded format and texture counts. |

Bake the terrain mask, scan tinting, ripples and displacement detail to UV-mapped base color, tangent normal and packed occlusion/roughness/metallic textures before export. Preserve dune crests and the visible cliff silhouette during decimation. Use shared mesh buffers and instance batches, reduced detail for distant shrubs, and masked foliage with tuned mip coverage. The densest talus meshes are tens of thousands of triangles per instance in Blender. Begin with 200–500 triangles for visible mid-range pieces and represent distant gravel in normal and color atlases. Verify Blender export of alpha and tangent orientation with a separate test file. Measure GLB compressed size, decode time, draw calls, triangles, GPU texture residency and 95th-percentile frame interval on a physical phone and a desktop before locking budgets. Device tiers and network costs are estimates.
