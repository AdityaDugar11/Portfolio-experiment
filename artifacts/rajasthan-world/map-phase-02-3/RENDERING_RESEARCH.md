# Phase 02.3 rendering research

Use a single image with bounded depth reprojection for the miniature. For the transition to the street, source the aerial, exterior, approach, and threshold from a shared, registered Junagarh scene. Compare a short prerendered bridge against live projection on the same proxy before choosing a delivery format.

## Rendering techniques

The mobile implications below describe each technique. The checkpoint measures the 2.5D prototype on this workstation.

| Technique | Fidelity and usable camera motion | Asset work, mobile cost, and rights |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three.js image projection | A calibrated camera can project a still onto a plane or several receiver surfaces. The original pose retains the photograph's detail. One plane gives a planar push. Geometry adds perspective but only the surfaces visible to the projector contain valid color. | A single image and one simple draw are cheap to render. Decoded textures still occupy GPU memory. Three.js is already installed under MIT. Color assets require their own reuse rights. [PerspectiveCamera](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PerspectiveCamera.html) · [Texture](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Texture.html) |
| 2.5D depth-map parallax | Back-project RGB pixels through estimated depth, then move a perspective camera. Foreground, wall tops, and the floor move at different rates. Small shifts hold together. Larger shifts stretch cliffs and corners where the photograph never recorded the hidden surfaces. | One RGB asset plus one depth map. Monocular depth can run offline on a GPU or CPU. This prototype uses the Apache-2.0 Small checkpoint. The depth is relative and usually needs edge review. [Depth Anything V2 Small](https://huggingface.co/depth-anything/Depth-Anything-V2-Small-hf) · [3D Ken Burns paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05483) |
| Layered image planes | Hand-matted foreground ramparts, midground terrain, and backdrop can separate cleanly. Cutouts behave like cards at oblique angles and can expose cut edges. The floor and background must continue behind the cutouts. | Local masking and paint work. Several transparent planes increase overdraw, decoded texture memory, and alpha-edge work on mobile. License every source and any inpaint model independently. [Layered depth inpainting research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04727) |
| Depth-image-based rendering (DIBR) | Warps one or more RGB-D views into the target camera. Multiple calibrated views or layered depth can cover some newly exposed pixels. Regions that no camera saw still need authored or inferred fill. Closely spaced anchor views can preserve façade and roofline detail across more translation than the single-image proof. | Requires registered color and depth, visibility masks, and checked blending. Multi-view textures and reprojection passes increase upload and fill cost. A local toolchain is feasible. Rights must cover all views, derived assets, and any selected model. [3D Ken Burns paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05483) · [Layered depth inpainting research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04727) |
| Scroll-controlled image sequence | A frame index follows progress in either direction. A continuous dolly rendered from one scene can convincingly go from air to ground. The visitor is limited to the rendered track and crops. | Blender can render locally with fixed camera and light. A 120-frame 1280 × 720 RGBA sequence would occupy about 422 MiB if fully decoded. Encode responsive variants, prefetch a small window around the playhead, and evict distant frames. Asset rights carry through to the rendered sequence. [FFmpeg image sequence format](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-formats.html#image2) |
| Scroll-scrubbed cinematic video | The same rendered track can offer high visual detail at a lower transfer cost. Browser seeks are asynchronous. Long intervals between keyframes and reverse or random jumps can make scrubbing lag, even if linear playback looks excellent. | Encode locally with FFmpeg. Compare a short keyframe interval and a frame cache on the same physical phone. Measure seeks, decoded buffers, and bandwidth. Video duration, codec, keyframe interval, and source rights all matter. [HTML media seeking](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/HTMLMediaElement/currentTime) · [FFmpeg video encoder options](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-codecs.html) |
| Panoramic environment | A 360° plate supports looking around a fixed nodal point with a convincing outside view. A second nodal point needs a registered connecting shot or proxy to give the impression of walking. Moving the camera inside a sphere cannot recover accurate close parallax. | Capture or render from one calibrated station per panorama. Full equirectangular images are expensive at the detail needed for a gateway on mobile. Tiles or lower-resolution variants can bound eager memory. Use project-owned or licensed images, including any captured street imagery. [Blender panoramic camera manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/object_settings/cameras.html) |
| Gaussian splatting | A well-covered, calibrated set of views can reproduce irregular masonry and view-dependent detail within its capture volume. Sparse images, very wide changes in scale, foliage and never-seen regions can produce floating fragments or blank areas. The two illustrations do not supply multi-view coverage. | Requires coordinated access to enough overlapping capture or rendered views, camera calibration, local training, and a browser viewer. Sorting, blending, decoded splat buffers and fill rate need measurement on phones. The original INRIA implementation has a research/evaluation license. Apache-2.0 gsplat is a separate training option. Individual capture, viewer and splat rights still apply. [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04079) · [Original implementation license](https://github.com/graphdeco-inria/gaussian-splatting/blob/main/LICENSE.md) · [gsplat](https://github.com/nerfstudio-project/gsplat) |
| Proxy geometry with photographic projection | Model only the surfaces the camera passes or sees in silhouette. Register several photographs to the same wall, bastion, and ground. It supports selectable viewpoints within coverage and lets the same structure appear in offline renders. A single photo still cannot cover the back of a bastion or the underside of an arch. | Local Blender UV Project and Three.js suffice. Labor is in the camera solves, masks and lighting continuity. A baked texture atlas, one proxy, and a small number of texture variants are easier to bound than a site-scale splat. Photos projected into public renders need suitable licenses. [Blender UV Project modifier](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/modify/uv_project.html) |

## Recommended asset workflow

1. **Register the overview.** Start from the existing ten site coordinates, state outline, and local projection. Build a landmark layout that retains north/south and east/west relationships, including the close Amber and Nahargarh pair. Keep the desert, mountains, settlements, and water in plausible places. Enlarge landmarks and compress distances in a documented art pass. Use a project-authored or explicitly licensed overview with desktop and portrait framing. [Canonical coordinates](../map-phase-01/data/chapters.json)
2. **Resolve Junagarh before producing shots.** Select the approach side and the exact gate with reference evidence. Find reuse-cleared or owner-owned overviews of the wall and courtyard, oblique images of the approach, and eye-level views that show the enclosure and gateway from overlapping angles. Use identifiable control points to solve camera positions. The prior street-elevation study records estimates for the bearing, roofline, and footprint registration. Its visible portions do not establish a matched chain from aerial to street. [Existing exterior evidence](../documentation/junagarh/exterior-v01/README.md)
3. **Create one exterior proxy and camera track.** Use the same Junagarh courtyard silhouette, curtain wall, bastion, portal, and approach grade at every scale. Projection or baking supplies surface detail. Use a shared sun direction, shadow softness, exposure, and color transform. Register source imagery first and reject insufficiently covered surfaces. Keep rights and attribution with every source. Leave other forts as part of the overview art for this workflow.
4. **Render five linked views.** Export Rajasthan overview, Junagarh regional aerial, the exterior, the eye-level approach, and the stationary view just outside the threshold. Export camera matrices, full-precision depth, and visibility/object masks with the color plates. Render both desktop and portrait framings from the same scene. At each handoff match the shared landmark silhouette and camera direction. A provisional workflow review can use five stills and an offline animatic before spending time on delivery encodes.
5. **Test the handoff with identical camera poses.** Use the relief plate for the opening. Transition at a matched view into a short prerendered dolly, then compare that clip with the same path rendered live on the photo-projected proxy. Inspect thin rooflines, ground contact, and gate alignment frame by frame and in reverse. Favor a small image-sequence cache if seeking the video cannot consistently land on the intended frame. Use a panorama only if there is a reason to look around from the final stationary point.
6. **Choose the mobile package after measurements.** Test forward and reverse from a cold cache on a physical phone, including Safari if available. Measure transfer, time to first detailed frame, seek-to-present latency, decoded and browser memory, and frame times. Retain the accessible still path when motion or WebGL is unavailable.
